# 00 — Vision & Core Loop

**Gridlock: Silicon Syndicate** · Game Design Document · v0.1 (15 Sep 2026)

> You are the mastermind behind a black-ops corporate syndicate. Outmaneuver rival
> megacorporations on a city-wide scale by seizing physical choke points, laying
> fiber-optic infrastructure, and executing hostile takeovers before rival corporate
> security even knows they've been breached.

---

## 1. One-paragraph pitch

A turn-based, hex-based 4X set inside a single sprawling megacity. Territory is not
land you own but **sectors your network can reach and sustain**. Every sector exists on
two layers at once: a **physical layer** (data centers, substations, conduit, security
outposts) and a **cyber layer** (the sector's subnet, its firewall, and every syndicate's
hidden presence inside it). Your empire runs on **Bandwidth**, generated by nodes and
carried by fiber you had to physically lay; overextend the cable and **packet loss** eats
your control from the edges inward. You cannot see what your network does not touch.

## 2. The three pillars, stated as rules

| Pillar | Design rule (every feature must pass at least one) |
|---|---|
| **Information as Munition** | A sector you have no presence in shows **stale data** stamped with the cycle it was last seen. Scanning, surveillance and intrusion are the primary "combat" verbs. Being seen has a cost (Exposure). |
| **The Dual-Layer Battlefield** | Physical control without cyber presence is **blind**; cyber presence without physical reach is **starved** (no bandwidth to act with). Taking a sector for keeps requires both. |
| **The Bandwidth Economy** | Bandwidth is generated, **routed over a real graph**, lost per hop, and consumed by control, operations and research. Capital buys infrastructure; Bandwidth makes it *do* anything. |

## 3. Glossary (canonical terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cycle** | One turn. Roughly one in-world week. A standard game runs 150–200 cycles. |
| **Sector** | One hex of the city map. Has a type, a population, a conduit cost, a subnet. |
| **Node** | A data center. Generates raw Bandwidth. Needs Power. Has a Firewall. |
| **Link** | A fiber (or wireless) connection along a chain of hexes. Has capacity, upkeep, loss. |
| **Bandwidth (BW)** | Network throughput. Produced by nodes, delivered along links, consumed by sectors, ops, research. Not stockpiled (except a small Buffer). |
| **Capital (¤)** | Money. Stockpiled. Buys infrastructure, pays upkeep, bribes, buys shares. |
| **Power (MW)** | Produced by Substations or bought from the city grid. A node with no power outputs zero. |
| **Control Integrity (C)** | 0–100 per sector. Rises when the sector's bandwidth demand is met, decays when it is not. Yields scale with it. At 0 the sector goes neutral. |
| **Presence (P)** | 0–100 per (syndicate, sector subnet). Your cyber foothold. Grants vision, skimming, sabotage, and ultimately root. |
| **Firewall (F)** | 0–100 per subnet. Resists Presence gain. Owner can harden it. |
| **Exposure (X)** | 0–100 per syndicate. "Heat." High Exposure attracts the Bureau (audits, raids) and reveals your assets to everyone. |
| **Exchange** | Special neutral sector: a peering point to the city Backbone. Sell surplus BW, buy BW, reach distant sectors, and launch end-game operations. |
| **The Bureau** | Neutral municipal authority. Not a rival; a weather system. Reacts to Exposure, protects Exchanges, holds the Singularity kill switch. |
| **Syndicate** | A player or AI corporation. Has an archetype (see 02), an HQ "Crown Node", a Board, and Executives. |

## 4. The map

- **~400–600 hexes** for a standard city; 4–6 syndicates.
- Sector types (full table in 01): Financial District, Corporate Campus, Industrial,
  Port/Logistics, Arcology (dense residential), Sprawl, Undercity, Exchange, and
  **barrier hexes** (river, elevated rail, freeway) that carry conduit only at a crossing
  premium. Barriers create the choke points the fantasy is built on.
- **3–5 Exchanges**, never adjacent to any start, always behind at least one barrier.
- Each syndicate starts with: HQ hex containing a **Crown Node** (Tier 2), one
  Substation, 3 controlled adjacent sectors, 600¤, and a fog-of-war radius of 2.

## 5. Cycle structure

Players issue orders **simultaneously** during Planning. Resolution is deterministic and
ordered, so that the cause-and-effect chain is always the same and multiplayer is
lockstep-safe.

```
PLANNING  (all syndicates, simultaneous, timer optional)
   build / lay fiber / queue ops / set research allocation / market orders

RESOLUTION (fixed order)
   1. Power      — substations + grid purchases → which nodes are lit
   2. Flow       — max-flow from lit nodes over links; loss applied per hop
                   → delivered BW per sector, BW available for ops & research
   3. Cyber ops  — intrusions, hardening, scans, siphons, roots  (order: defense first)
   4. Physical   — construction completes, sabotage, raids, seizures
   5. Control    — Control Integrity updates from delivered/demand ratio
   6. Yields     — Capital from sectors × integrity; skims paid to intruders
   7. Upkeep     — nodes, links, outposts, grid power; shortfall → forced brownouts
   8. Market     — Exchange BW sales/purchases; share prices update
   9. Exposure   — heat gained/cooled; Bureau reactions
  10. Events     — board reviews, city events, victory checks
```

Why this order matters: **Power gates Flow, Flow gates everything else.** A single
successful physical raid on a substation (step 4, *last* cycle) darkens a node at step 1
*this* cycle, which starves the ops that node was funding at step 3. Players feel the
dual layer as a pipeline, not two separate minigames.

## 6. The core loop, in the player's words

1. **Scout.** Spend a little BW to scan neighboring subnets. Learn what's there and
   what it costs to reach.
2. **Lay cable.** Buy or seize conduit rights hex by hex toward something valuable.
   Watch the projected loss meter as the line gets long.
3. **Light it up.** Build a node or repeater at the far end so the new sectors are fed,
   not starved. Control Integrity climbs. Capital flows.
4. **Get inside.** Push Presence into a rival's subnets along the border. Now you can
   *see* them. Skim their yields. Induce loss on the links you can touch.
5. **Take it.** When a rival sector browns out, claim it: lay a line in, and root the
   subnet. Physical + cyber, both, or it doesn't stick.
6. **Don't get caught.** Every op adds Exposure. The Bureau audits the loud. Rivals
   who see your compute signature start counting the cycles to your victory.
7. **Choose your ending** (see 03). Buy the city, upload a god, cut every rival's
   lights at once, or make it all legal.

## 7. What makes it *not* Civilization-with-neon

- **Territory is derived, not claimed.** You never "own" a hex; you sustain it. Cut the
  cable and the map recolors itself within three cycles.
- **Fog is the default and vision is purchased per cycle.** Surveillance has upkeep.
- **Two health bars per sector** (Integrity and Firewall) that different tools attack.
- **The best defense is topology.** A ring beats a chain. A repeater in the right barrier
  crossing is worth more than an army.
- **Victories are asymmetric and asymmetric to *counter*.** Each ending has a distinct
  tell and a distinct set of counters, and AI rivals only see the tells their own
  networks reach.
